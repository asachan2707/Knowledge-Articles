Forward Deployed Engineer: The Best AI Job for New Grads?

Forward Deployed Engineers (FDEs) live inside client organizations for 3-month engagements, building custom AI solutions on the client's systems with the client's data. Job postings surged +800% from January to September 2025. Here is what the role actually looks like day-to-day, which companies are hiring, and whether it is the right path for you.

Domain: Career / AI / Engineering Published: 2026-07-16

Most new grad engineering roles have you maintaining a feature for six months before you touch anything interesting. The Forward Deployed Engineer role inverts this: you are building something real for a paying client in your first week, in an industry you may have never worked in, with a codebase you have never seen. It is the fastest learning environment in software engineering right now — and it is also not right for everyone.

By the numbers: FDE job postings grew +800% from January to September 2025. Salesforce committed to hiring 1,000 FDEs. OpenAI and Palantir are hiring aggressively. Dozens of AI startups have adopted the model. Salaries range from $130k to $220k depending on company and experience. This is a job category in formation, and being early matters.

Story · The 3-month engagement

Day 1. Nadia, a 23-year-old FDE six months out of college, is sitting in a conference room at a regional bank's headquarters. She has been assigned to implement an AI loan processing assistant directly inside the bank's Salesforce environment. She has two weeks of Agentforce certification from Salesforce's "Ready in Six" onboarding program. She knows the platform. She does not know anything about loans.

The first week is mostly listening. She sits with loan officers, watches how they manually cross-reference applicant data from four different systems, asks why certain fields matter and what they are checking for. By the end of week one she can draw the loan officer's workflow from memory. The pod strategist — a senior Salesforce consultant on the same engagement — is already managing the stakeholder relationships and timeline. Nadia owns the technical build.

Day 30. The first prototype is running on real anonymized data. It is not perfect — the AI occasionally flags applications for review that an experienced loan officer would approve immediately — but the client can see it working. The loan officers are engaged, suggesting improvements. The feedback loop between "what the AI does" and "what a human would do" is compressing fast.

Day 90. The bank's loan officers are spending 60% less time on manual data entry. The AI handles the routine cases. Humans handle the edge cases and the final approval decision. Nadia writes her handoff documentation, trains two bank-side developers on how to maintain and extend the system, and moves to her next engagement: a healthcare company in another city.

She has now worked in banking and healthcare. By year two she will have worked in six industries.

What Is a Forward Deployed Engineer

Role Definition and Origins

A Forward Deployed Engineer is a hybrid: part software engineer, part solutions architect, part consultant. The core model — deploying technical staff into client organizations to build and configure software directly on the client's systems — was pioneered by Palantir for government and defense contracts starting in the late 2000s. The logic was simple: enterprise software is expensive to customize remotely. If you put an engineer inside the client, they learn the problem fast, build in context, and ship something that actually works.

The model has spread to enterprise software companies and AI-native companies because AI products have the same challenge at higher stakes: an AI system trained on the wrong data or configured for the wrong workflow is worse than no AI at all. Getting it right requires being close to the client's actual systems and actual users.

FDE vs. Sales Engineer (SE): A Sales Engineer builds demos and proofs of concept to support the sales process. The demo is disposable — it gets the deal signed. An FDE builds production systems that the client runs after the engagement ends. The FDE's code ships. This distinction matters because it changes the quality bar and the scope of what you are responsible for.

Where FDEs Work

The Role at Different Companies

Salesforce (Agentforce): The largest FDE hiring program in 2025–2026. Salesforce committed to 1,000 FDEs to support their Agentforce platform — AI agents that run inside Salesforce CRM environments. The pod model: one strategist (senior consultant who manages the client relationship and engagement scope) plus two FDEs (who do the technical build). Engagement length: approximately 3 months. The "Ready in Six" program is Salesforce's 6-week onboarding for new FDEs — the fastest structured onboarding in the industry. Salary range: $130k–$180k for new grads with the program.

OpenAI: Enterprise custom implementations of the OpenAI API and custom GPTs inside client environments. OpenAI FDEs work at the frontier: clients who want to use the most advanced models before they are widely available, building integrations that require deep understanding of model capabilities and limitations. Typically smaller engagements in terms of client count, larger in terms of technical complexity. Salary range: $160k–$220k.

Palantir: The original model, still running. Palantir engagements are longer (6–12 months), deeper (entire data infrastructure plus AI layer), and more complex (often in government, defense, and healthcare where data governance and security requirements are severe). Palantir FDEs often specialize in a domain and do fewer total engagements but go deeper into each. Palantir's culture is notoriously intense; the FDE role there is considered one of the most demanding in the industry. Salary range: $140k–$200k.

AI startups: Dozens of enterprise AI startups — document intelligence, customer support automation, sales intelligence, compliance automation — have adopted the FDE model for their enterprise deployments. Engagements may be shorter (4–8 weeks), equity upside is higher, platform depth is more variable. Good option for engineers who want broader ownership and faster learning at the cost of less institutional support.

Core Skills

The 5 Skills That Make or Break an FDE

1. Software Engineering

Production-quality code, written fast, in codebases you have never seen before. You cannot spend a week reading the codebase — you need to be functional in it on day two. This means strong fundamentals: API design, error handling, authentication patterns, data modeling. You will also frequently be debugging in client environments you do not control — a Salesforce org with custom objects you cannot change, a legacy CRM with API quirks, an internal API with no documentation. The ability to work with constraints you did not set is core.

2. LLM and AI Product Knowledge

You need to know your platform deeply. For a Salesforce FDE, this means Agentforce: how agents are configured, how they call Salesforce Flows, how Apex classes integrate with LLM outputs, how to manage the data model for AI features. For an OpenAI FDE, this means the API's full surface: assistants, function calling, fine-tuning trade-offs, knowledge retrieval. Beyond the platform, you need a working mental model of RAG pipelines, prompt engineering, and hallucination mitigation — because the client will ask "why did it say that?" and you need a real answer.

3. Client Communication

This is the skill most engineers underestimate. You will be presenting progress demos to non-technical executives every two weeks. You will be fielding "can you just add X" requests that would require rearchitecting the solution. You will be delivering bad news — "this feature will take three weeks, not three days" — without losing the client's trust. The ability to translate between business intent and technical reality, speak at the right level of detail for each audience, and manage expectations under pressure is as important as writing good code.

4. Rapid Prototyping

The FDE rhythm: demo in 2 days, iterate based on feedback, ship in 2 weeks. This is not waterfall engineering with a two-week sprint cycle — it is continuous delivery of partial functionality to an audience that gives you real feedback every 48 hours. MVP thinking applied to enterprise: what is the smallest thing I can build that demonstrates the core value and gives the client something concrete to react to? The ability to cut scope aggressively — and explain why the cut is right — is essential.

5. Domain Adaptability

You learn a new industry every 3 months. Banking, then insurance, then healthcare, then retail. Each domain has its own vocabulary, its own regulatory constraints, its own definition of a "good" outcome for an AI system. The skill is not becoming an expert in each domain in 3 months — it is learning to ask the right questions fast. What does "successful" look like for this workflow? What are the consequences of an incorrect AI output? What data exists and what data is missing? Who are the users, and what do they trust?

The Salesforce Model

How "Ready in Six" Works

Salesforce's 6-week onboarding program for new FDEs is one of the most structured accelerated onboardings in the industry. The design philosophy: learning by doing beats months of classroom training. Every week has a different learning modality, escalating in real responsibility.

  • Week 1 — Platform deep-dive: Agentforce architecture, Salesforce data model, Flows, Apex integration patterns, the FDE's toolbox. Intensive. You emerge with functional platform knowledge.
  • Week 2 — Mock client engagement (internal): Simulated engagement with internal Salesforce employees playing the client role. You build a small AI feature end-to-end. You present a demo. You handle "can you add X" requests.
  • Week 3 — Shadow a senior FDE on a real engagement: Observe how the senior handles client interactions, scope management, technical decision-making under client constraints. Ask questions after hours, not during client sessions.
  • Week 4 — Lead a component of a real engagement: You own one specific deliverable within an active engagement. The senior FDE is still present. You write the code, present the demo, handle questions. The senior catches you if you fall.
  • Week 5 — Full engagement responsibility with support: You are the primary technical contact. The senior FDE is available but not present in every meeting. You manage the client relationship and the technical build.
  • Week 6 — Retrospective and next assignment: What did you learn? What would you do differently? What skills do you need to develop? Then: your first fully independent engagement assignment.

This model works because it compresses years of consultant learning into 6 weeks through deliberate exposure to real stakes. By week 6 you have already handled client feedback, scope change requests, and a live demo under pressure. The first independent engagement is harder, but you are not encountering these challenges for the first time.

Day in the Life

What Tuesday Looks Like

9:00am — Client standup (30 min): Three questions: what did you build yesterday, what are you building today, what is blocking you? For an FDE, "blocking" often means "the data I expected to be in this Salesforce field is not there" or "the API endpoint I planned to use requires a permission the client's admin has not granted yet." The standup is also relationship maintenance — the loan officers are your users and your QA team simultaneously.

9:30am–12:00pm — Build session: The core work. For a Salesforce engagement this might be: wiring an Agentforce flow to a custom Apex class that queries an external underwriting API, then formatting the response for display in the loan officer's Lightning page. You are debugging in the Salesforce developer console, testing with sandbox data, and iterating on the prompts that tell the agent how to handle edge cases.

12:00pm — Lunch with client team: This is not optional social time. Relationships with the client team are the mechanism by which you get access to the domain knowledge and the organizational context you need to build the right thing. The loan officer who explains over lunch why certain applicant behaviors are red flags that do not appear in the data model is giving you information that will save you a week of rework.

1:00pm–4:00pm — Build session + debugging: Often the afternoon involves something that was supposed to be simple and is not. The client's sandbox environment behaves differently from the documentation. The external API returns a field in a format that does not match what the AI agent expects. You spend an hour on what you thought would take ten minutes.

4:00pm — Demo progress to client stakeholder (30 min): A brief demo of whatever you completed today. Even if it is partial. Showing early progress builds trust and surfaces requirements gaps early — the stakeholder who says "that is not quite what I meant" on day 15 saves you from a larger correction on day 89.

4:30pm–5:30pm — Internal sync with pod strategist: Scope review, risk assessment, stakeholder dynamics. The strategist manages the relationship at the executive level; you bring the technical picture. Together you decide what gets cut, what gets escalated, and what the narrative is for the next client review.

Evening (sometimes): Reading the client's domain documentation. The loan compliance regulations you did not understand this morning are explained in a 40-page PDF that the loan officer mentioned in passing. You read it.

Is It Right for You?

Honest Assessment: Pros, Cons, Who Thrives

Why new grads choose FDE roles:

  • Extremely fast learning: New domain every 3 months, breadth of real production problems, exposure to how large enterprises actually use software
  • High visibility early in career: You present to C-suite stakeholders in your first year. Most engineers never do this in their first five years.
  • Salary premium: FDE roles pay 20–40% above comparable software engineering roles at the same experience level
  • Strong career brand: Salesforce, OpenAI, or Palantir on your resume, with "built and deployed AI solutions for enterprise clients" is a differentiated story
  • AI product depth: You will know more about how AI products work in real enterprise environments than most ML researchers, because you are building them under real constraints

Why some engineers are wrong for the role:

  • Travel: Depending on the company, 30–50% travel. Some engagements are remote; others require on-site presence. If you hate travel or have constraints that make it impractical, this role is difficult.
  • Context-switching cost: Every 3 months you abandon deep context and start from zero. If you need months to do your best work, the FDE cadence is frustrating.
  • Client politics: Clients have stakeholders who disagree. You will build something one stakeholder loves and another hates. Navigating this without organizational power requires a specific kind of patience.
  • No deep ownership: You hand off and move on. If the idea of building something and then watching someone else evolve it for years sounds unsatisfying, FDE will leave you with that feeling consistently.

Who thrives in FDE roles: Intellectually curious engineers who get more energy from novelty than from depth. People who communicate clearly and enjoy explaining technical concepts to non-technical audiences. Engineers who handle ambiguity without needing to fully understand the problem before they start building. People who see client relationships as interesting rather than overhead.

Getting Hired

How to Land an FDE Role

Required: Strong software engineering fundamentals — data structures, APIs, system design basics, ability to navigate unfamiliar codebases. Plus depth in at least one AI platform or framework: Salesforce Agentforce, OpenAI API, LangChain, or similar. The combination of "can write production code" and "understands LLM systems" is the minimum threshold.

Valued but not required: Any consulting, freelance, or client-facing engineering experience. Demonstrated ability to demo under pressure (a portfolio of projects you can walk through, live, without preparing the demo in advance). Domain knowledge in high-value enterprise verticals (banking, insurance, healthcare, legal).

The interview process typically has three stages:

  1. Technical coding round: Standard LeetCode-style or take-home. Nothing unusual here — prepare as you would for any software engineering role.
  2. System design: Often AI-specific. "Design a document QA system for a law firm" or "design an AI customer support system for a bank." Focus on how you would handle data privacy, evaluation, and handoff to human agents — these show enterprise thinking.
  3. Client scenario: The differentiating round. You are given a vague business problem: "A hospital wants to use AI to reduce administrative burden on nurses." You have 20 minutes to scope it: what are you building, what data does it need, what does MVP look like, what are the risks, what questions do you need answered before starting? This round is entirely about your judgment and communication under ambiguity.

The single most effective interview preparation: Build a personal project using the company's platform and be able to demo it live. A working Agentforce flow running in your personal Salesforce developer org, or a working OpenAI API integration deployed on a free-tier cloud provider, demonstrates all the relevant skills simultaneously: you know the platform, you can build, you can explain what you built and why you made the choices you did.

The client scenario interview tip: The most common mistake candidates make in FDE role-play rounds is immediately launching into a technical answer. The right instinct is to ask a clarifying question first — "Before I answer, can you help me understand the current workflow?" This is exactly what an effective FDE does on day one of a real engagement. Interviewers notice the difference between candidates who perform knowledge and candidates who seek understanding. Seek understanding first.

Career Trajectory

Where FDE Roles Lead

The FDE role is a career accelerant. Most engineers who do it for 2–3 years do not stay in it permanently — not because it stops being valuable, but because it opens doors that are hard to open otherwise.

  • Year 1: FDE building confidence across 3–4 engagements. By the end of year one you have seen more enterprise AI implementations than most engineers see in a decade.
  • Year 2: Senior FDE leading pods, mentoring junior FDEs, owning the client relationship for entire engagements. The strategist role becomes accessible.
  • Year 3: FDE Manager (manage a team of FDEs across multiple concurrent engagements), Solutions Architect (platform design for enterprise deployments), or Product Manager (you understand enterprise AI requirements better than most PMs).

Common exit paths:

  • The client hires you: After a successful engagement, clients frequently try to hire the FDE who built their system. The salary step-up is real and the domain expertise you built during the engagement transfers.
  • Product management: FDEs who discover they care more about "what to build" than "how to build it" are unusually good PM candidates — they understand technical constraints, client needs, and how to scope.
  • AI consultancy: Starting an independent consultancy after 2–3 years as an FDE, with a client network and a portfolio of real enterprise AI implementations, is a viable and well-trodden path.
JavaScript Example

A Typical FDE Deliverable: CRM Event Webhook Handler

JavaScript Example 
/**
 * A representative FDE deliverable: a webhook handler that receives a CRM event
 * (new lead created in Salesforce), calls an LLM to generate a personalized
 * outreach message draft, and posts the draft back to the CRM via API.
 *
 * This pattern — CRM event → LLM enrichment → CRM update — is one of the
 * most common FDE deliverables in sales and marketing automation engagements.
 *
 * Stack: Node.js + Express, Anthropic Claude API, Salesforce REST API
 */

import express   from 'express';
import Anthropic from '@anthropic-ai/sdk';

const app       = express();
const anthropic = new Anthropic({ apiKey: process.env.ANTHROPIC_API_KEY });

app.use(express.json());

// ── POST /webhook/lead-created ───────────────────────────────────────────────
// Salesforce sends this when a new Lead record is created.
app.post('/webhook/lead-created', async (req, res) => {
  // Validate webhook signature (omitted for brevity — always do this in production)
  const { leadId, firstName, lastName, company, jobTitle, leadSource, industry } = req.body;

  console.log(`New lead: ${firstName} ${lastName} at ${company} (${leadId})`);

  try {
    // ── Step 1: Generate personalized outreach draft via LLM ─────────────────
    const outreachDraft = await generateOutreachDraft({
      firstName, lastName, company, jobTitle, leadSource, industry,
    });

    // ── Step 2: Post the draft back to Salesforce as a Task ──────────────────
    await createSalesforceTask({
      leadId,
      subject:     `AI Draft: Outreach to ${firstName} ${lastName}`,
      description: outreachDraft,
      type:        'Email',
      status:      'Not Started', // Rep reviews and edits before sending
    });

    console.log(`Outreach draft created for lead ${leadId}`);
    res.status(200).json({ success: true, leadId });

  } catch (error) {
    console.error(`Failed to process lead ${leadId}:`, error.message);
    // Return 200 to prevent Salesforce from retrying (we'll handle retries internally)
    // In production: push to a dead-letter queue for manual review
    res.status(200).json({ success: false, error: error.message, leadId });
  }
});

// ── LLM: Generate personalized outreach message ──────────────────────────────
async function generateOutreachDraft({ firstName, lastName, company, jobTitle, leadSource, industry }) {
  const message = await anthropic.messages.create({
    model:      'claude-haiku-20240307', // Fast and cheap for high-volume lead processing
    max_tokens: 400,
    system: `You are a sales assistant generating personalized first-touch outreach emails.
Write in a professional but conversational tone. Be specific and concise.
Do NOT use generic phrases like "I hope this finds you well" or "I wanted to reach out."
The email should feel like it was written by a human who did their research.
Output ONLY the email body — no subject line, no signature.`,
    messages: [
      {
        role:    'user',
        content: `Write a personalized first-touch outreach email for this lead:

Name: ${firstName} ${lastName}
Job Title: ${jobTitle || 'not specified'}
Company: ${company}
Industry: ${industry || 'not specified'}
How they found us: ${leadSource || 'not specified'}

The email should:
1. Open with a specific, relevant hook (based on their industry or role)
2. Clearly state what we offer and why it is relevant to them specifically
3. Include a single, low-friction call to action (a 15-minute call)
Keep it under 150 words.`,
      },
    ],
  });

  return message.content[0].text;
}

// ── Salesforce REST API: Create a Task linked to the Lead ────────────────────
async function createSalesforceTask({ leadId, subject, description, type, status }) {
  // Get an access token (uses OAuth 2.0 client credentials flow)
  const token = await getSalesforceAccessToken();

  const response = await fetch(
    `${process.env.SALESFORCE_INSTANCE_URL}/services/data/v59.0/sobjects/Task`,
    {
      method:  'POST',
      headers: {
        'Authorization': `Bearer ${token}`,
        'Content-Type':  'application/json',
      },
      body: JSON.stringify({
        WhoId:       leadId,    // Links task to the Lead record
        Subject:     subject,
        Description: description,
        Type:        type,
        Status:      status,
        OwnerId:     process.env.SALESFORCE_DEFAULT_OWNER_ID, // Assigned to sales rep
      }),
    }
  );

  if (!response.ok) {
    const error = await response.json();
    throw new Error(`Salesforce Task creation failed: ${JSON.stringify(error)}`);
  }

  return await response.json();
}

// ── Salesforce OAuth 2.0 (simplified — production should cache the token) ─────
async function getSalesforceAccessToken() {
  const response = await fetch(
    `${process.env.SALESFORCE_INSTANCE_URL}/services/oauth2/token`,
    {
      method: 'POST',
      headers: { 'Content-Type': 'application/x-www-form-urlencoded' },
      body: new URLSearchParams({
        grant_type:    'client_credentials',
        client_id:     process.env.SALESFORCE_CLIENT_ID,
        client_secret: process.env.SALESFORCE_CLIENT_SECRET,
      }),
    }
  );
  const data = await response.json();
  return data.access_token;
}

app.listen(3000, () => console.log('Lead webhook handler running on port 3000'));

/**
 * What makes this a good FDE deliverable:
 *
 * 1. It solves a real client problem (manual outreach drafting takes 15 min/lead;
 *    this takes 3 seconds and the rep just edits and sends)
 *
 * 2. It keeps humans in the loop (the AI draft goes into a Task, not directly into
 *    an email — the sales rep reviews, edits, and sends. This is the right design
 *    for a first implementation.)
 *
 * 3. It uses the client's existing systems (Salesforce Task) rather than introducing
 *    a new workflow — lower adoption friction
 *
 * 4. It is scoped for a 2-day prototype (this is the core of the feature; production
 *    would add token caching, retry logic, more sophisticated personalization using
 *    LinkedIn data or company website content)
 *
 * 5. It is explainable to a non-technical executive: "When a new lead comes in,
 *    AI writes a personalized email draft. Your rep reviews it, clicks edit if needed,
 *    and sends. We're seeing 40% faster first-touch time."
 */
JavaScript Example

Rapid Prototype Pattern: AI Feature Planner from Business Requirement

JavaScript Example 
/**
 * Rapid Prototype Pattern: AI Feature Planner
 *
 * FDEs often use an LLM to translate a business requirement (captured during
 * a discovery call) into a structured implementation plan before writing any
 * product code. This is an internal planning tool — it produces a JSON plan
 * with components, integration points, risks, and an effort estimate that the
 * FDE can present to the client in the same afternoon.
 *
 * Input:  plain-English business requirement + optional client context
 * Output: structured JSON implementation plan
 *
 * This pattern lets an FDE produce a credible technical plan within 15 minutes
 * of a discovery call, rather than going silent for a day to "think it over."
 *
 * Stack: Node.js, Anthropic Claude API with structured JSON output
 */

import Anthropic from '@anthropic-ai/sdk';

const anthropic = new Anthropic({ apiKey: process.env.ANTHROPIC_API_KEY });

// ── System prompt for the FDE planner ────────────────────────────────────────
const SYSTEM_PROMPT = `You are a Forward Deployed Engineer at an enterprise AI company.
You have just completed a discovery call with a client. Based on a business requirement,
you produce structured implementation plans that are:
- Technically specific (name real technologies, APIs, and integration patterns)
- Realistic about effort (enterprise integrations take longer than greenfield)
- Honest about risk (data access and security approval are always the main blockers)
- Scoped to what can be delivered in a standard 3-month FDE engagement

Always consider: data availability, security/compliance constraints, existing systems
to integrate with, and what the MVP looks like versus the full vision.`;

// ── Generate a structured implementation plan ────────────────────────────────
async function planAiFeature(requirement, clientContext = '') {
  const userMessage = clientContext
    ? `Client context:\n${clientContext}\n\nBusiness requirement:\n${requirement}`
    : `Business requirement:\n${requirement}`;

  const response = await anthropic.messages.create({
    model:      'claude-opus-4-5',
    max_tokens: 1500,
    system:     SYSTEM_PROMPT,
    messages:   [
      {
        role:    'user',
        content: `${userMessage}

Return a JSON object with this exact structure:
{
  "featureName": "short descriptive name",
  "problemStatement": "one sentence: the core business problem being solved",
  "proposedSolution": "one paragraph: the technical approach at a high level",
  "components": [
    {
      "name": "component name",
      "description": "what it does",
      "technology": "recommended stack/service/API",
      "effortDays": 
    }
  ],
  "integrationPoints": [
    {
      "system": "external system name",
      "accessType": "read|write|read-write",
      "riskLevel": "low|medium|high",
      "notes": "what makes this easy or hard"
    }
  ],
  "risks": [
    {
      "risk": "description of the risk",
      "likelihood": "low|medium|high",
      "mitigation": "how to address it"
    }
  ],
  "totalEffortDays": ,
  "fitsIn90Days": ,
  "mvpScope": "if the full scope is too large, what is the smallest useful MVP?"
}

Return only the JSON object — no markdown, no code fences, no explanation.`,
      },
    ],
  });

  // Parse the returned JSON
  const text = response.content[0].text.trim();
  return JSON.parse(text);
}

// ── Format the plan for a client-facing summary ──────────────────────────────
function formatPlanSummary(plan) {
  const lines = [
    `FEATURE: ${plan.featureName}`,
    `PROBLEM: ${plan.problemStatement}`,
    ``,
    `APPROACH: ${plan.proposedSolution}`,
    ``,
    `COMPONENTS (${plan.totalEffortDays} engineering days total):`,
    ...plan.components.map(c =>
      `  • ${c.name} [${c.effortDays}d] — ${c.technology}`
    ),
    ``,
    `INTEGRATION RISKS:`,
    ...plan.integrationPoints
      .filter(i => i.riskLevel !== 'low')
      .map(i => `  • ${i.system} (${i.riskLevel} risk): ${i.notes}`),
    ``,
    `FITS IN 90-DAY ENGAGEMENT: ${plan.fitsIn90Days ? 'Yes' : 'No — MVP scope: ' + plan.mvpScope}`,
  ];
  return lines.join('\n');
}

// ── Example: discovery call output from a regional bank ─────────────────────
const requirement = `
  Our loan officers receive financial statements from borrowers as PDF attachments
  in email. They currently re-key the key financial figures (revenue, EBITDA, total
  debt, cash on hand) manually into our loan origination system. This takes 3-4 hours
  per officer per day across a team of 200 officers.
  We want to automate the extraction and pre-population of these fields.
`;

const clientContext = `
  Mid-size regional commercial bank. 200 loan officers across 12 branches.
  CRM: Salesforce Sales Cloud. Loan origination: nCino (has a REST API).
  Document storage: SharePoint Online (Microsoft 365 tenant).
  Security requirement: all data must stay within their Azure tenant.
  No existing AI or ML infrastructure. IT security team requires a 2-week review
  cycle for any new API integration that touches production loan data.
`;

const plan = await planAiFeature(requirement, clientContext);
console.log(formatPlanSummary(plan));

/*
  Example output:

  FEATURE: Automated Financial Statement Extraction
  PROBLEM: Loan officers spend 3-4 hours/day manually rekeying borrower
           financial figures from PDFs into the loan origination system.

  APPROACH: Use Azure Document Intelligence (Form Recognizer) to extract
  structured data from financial statement PDFs stored in SharePoint,
  normalize the extracted fields against the nCino data model using an
  LLM-powered mapping layer, and pre-populate nCino loan records via the
  REST API. A lightweight review UI in Salesforce Lightning lets loan
  officers confirm or correct extracted values before write-back.

  COMPONENTS (19 engineering days total):
    • SharePoint → Azure Blob pipeline [2d] — Power Automate + Azure Logic Apps
    • Document extraction service [5d] — Azure Document Intelligence + custom model
    • LLM normalization layer [3d] — Claude API with structured output
    • nCino write-back integration [5d] — nCino REST API + OAuth 2.0
    • Salesforce review UI [4d] — Lightning Web Component

  INTEGRATION RISKS:
    • nCino REST API (high risk): Production write access requires 2-week security

Self-Assessment

Is FDE Right for You? — 10 Questions

  • Do you get more energy from learning something new than from mastering something familiar?
  • Are you comfortable presenting technical work to non-technical executives, even when the work is incomplete?
  • Can you write production-quality code in an unfamiliar codebase within 2 days of reading it?
  • Are you able to travel 30–50% of the time, or does your current situation make that impractical?
  • When you do not know something, do you ask questions quickly and without embarrassment?
  • Are you comfortable shipping an MVP that is 70% of the ideal solution because the engagement timeline requires it?
  • Can you handle stakeholder conflict — where two people on the client team want different things — without taking sides or escalating unnecessarily?
  • Do you have basic familiarity with at least one LLM API or AI platform (OpenAI API, Anthropic, Salesforce Agentforce)?
  • Are you willing to read industry documentation in the evenings to prepare for next-day client sessions?
  • Would you find it satisfying to hand off a completed system and move on, rather than owning it long-term?

If you answered yes to 7 or more: the FDE role is likely a strong fit. If you answered no to travel, stakeholder conflict, or handing off ownership: be honest with yourself about whether those are dealbreakers before applying.

FAQ

Common Questions

Do I need enterprise experience before applying for an FDE role?

No — the "Ready in Six" and similar onboarding programs are specifically designed for engineers without enterprise consulting backgrounds. What you need is strong software engineering fundamentals and the communication skills to work with clients. Enterprise knowledge comes from doing the engagements.

How much do FDE roles pay for new grads?

At Salesforce: approximately $130k–$150k base plus performance bonuses tied to engagement outcomes. At OpenAI: $160k–$180k. At Palantir: $140k–$165k plus significant equity. At AI startups: $110k–$160k with higher equity. Salaries are generally 20–40% above comparable software engineering new-grad roles, reflecting the client-facing nature and the travel requirement.

What happens if a client engagement goes badly?

Engagements fail — scope is misjudged, the client's data is worse than expected, a key stakeholder leaves mid-engagement. This is normal. FDE programs have escalation paths: the pod strategist manages client relationship recovery, and senior FDEs have seen most failure modes. Failure on an engagement is not career-ending; how you handle it and what you learn is what matters. Companies that run FDE programs expect some percentage of engagements to run into problems.

Can I transition from FDE to a product engineering role later?

Yes, and it is a common and valued transition. FDE engineers who move into product roles bring unusual strengths: deep understanding of how enterprises actually use software, direct experience with customer feedback loops, and the ability to scope and ship fast. The transition typically happens 2–4 years in, when engineers have built enough breadth that they want to go deep on one product.

Key Takeaways

The Forward Deployed Engineer role is the fastest learning environment in software engineering right now. Job postings grew +800% in 2025 and the hiring pace is accelerating. For engineers who are intellectually curious, communicate well, and are energized by novelty rather than depth, it is an exceptional early-career path: high salary, high visibility, and a career accelerant that opens doors most engineers do not see for five years. The key question is not whether you can do the technical work — most strong software engineers can. The question is whether the rhythm of 3-month engagements, client relationships, and constant domain switching is energizing or exhausting for you. Answer that honestly, and the decision becomes clear.
