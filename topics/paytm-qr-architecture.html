Paytm QR Code Architecture

How Paytm engineered a QR-based payment system capable of handling hundreds of millions of daily transactions — covering static vs. dynamic QR generation, the payment routing pipeline, idempotency guarantees, and the real-time settlement backbone.

Source: YouTube — System Design Deep Dive Domain: Payments / Fintech Published: 2024

India's UPI ecosystem processes over 10 billion transactions a month. Paytm's QR infrastructure sits at the entry point of that flow — every scan kicks off a chain of identity resolution, fraud checks, routing decisions, and ledger updates that must all complete in under 2 seconds. Building that at scale required rethinking every layer from QR generation to final settlement.

Core idea: A QR code is just a URL. The hard engineering is everything that happens after the scan — authenticating both parties, routing to the right bank, ensuring exactly-once debit/credit, and reconciling in real time across a distributed ledger.

Story · The chai stall problem

Imagine 50 million small merchants — chai stalls, auto-rickshaw drivers, vegetable vendors — each with a printed QR code stuck to a wall with tape. That QR cannot expire. It cannot rotate. It cannot carry session state. Yet when a customer scans it at 8 AM on a Tuesday and again at 3 PM on Friday, Paytm's system must resolve the correct merchant, apply any active cashback offer, prevent double charges, and credit the merchant's wallet — all within the 2-second UPI timeout window.

That constraint — static QR, dynamic transaction — shaped every architectural decision in the stack.

Layer 1

Static vs. Dynamic QR Generation

Paytm operates two QR modes with fundamentally different lifecycles:

  • Static QR (merchant QR): Generated once at merchant onboarding. Encodes a payee VPA (Virtual Payment Address) and an optional merchant ID. The QR itself never changes — it is a printed artefact. All dynamic data (amount, invoice reference, offer ID) is entered by the customer or resolved server-side on scan.
  • Dynamic QR (invoice QR): Generated per-transaction by the merchant's POS or Paytm Business app. Encodes a session token, pre-filled amount, and an expiry TTL (typically 5–15 minutes). Used by larger merchants (fuel pumps, restaurants, retail chains) where the POS controls the flow.

Static QRs account for the vast majority of Paytm's merchant base. The system must resolve the merchant's current account status, KYC tier, and settlement preferences at scan time — none of that is baked into the QR itself.

Layer 2

The Scan-to-Payment Pipeline

When a customer opens the Paytm app and scans a QR, the following sequence fires:

  1. QR decode & parse: The mobile client parses the QR payload (UPI deep-link format: upi://pay?pa=vpa&pn=name&am=amount&cu=INR). The VPA is the key lookup identifier.
  2. Merchant resolution: A read-heavy microservice maps the VPA to an internal merchant profile (account ID, KYC status, settlement account, active limits). This is served from an in-memory cache (Redis) backed by a primary store; cache TTL is short (~60 s) to pick up account suspensions in near real-time.
  3. Fraud & risk check: The transaction metadata (payer, payee, amount, device fingerprint, GPS, time-of-day) is evaluated by a risk engine before the payment is submitted to UPI. High-risk signals trigger step-up authentication (PIN re-entry, OTP).
  4. UPI routing: Paytm acts as a TPAP (Third Party Application Provider) on the UPI rails. The payment request is forwarded to the NPCI (National Payments Corporation of India) switch, which routes it to the payer's bank (debit) and payee's bank (credit). NPCI returns a transaction reference (TRN) and a final status.
  5. Callback & notification: NPCI sends a webhook to Paytm's callback service. The result is written to the transaction store, the merchant's passbook is updated, and both parties receive push notifications.
Layer 3

Idempotency and Exactly-Once Semantics

Network failures between Paytm and NPCI are the hardest problem. A request can succeed at NPCI but the success callback may never arrive — leaving Paytm's system uncertain whether money moved.

The solution is a two-phase idempotency design:

  • Idempotency key: Every payment initiation generates a UUID written to a distributed lock store (Redis with TTL) before the UPI call goes out. Duplicate scan events with the same key are rejected immediately.
  • Transaction status reconciliation: A background reconciliation job polls NPCI's Transaction Status API for all payments in a pending or unknown state older than 30 seconds. This is the safety net when callbacks are lost or delayed.
  • Debit-before-credit ordering: The system never marks a transaction as successful to either party until NPCI confirms both legs (debit from payer bank, credit to payee bank) are committed. Partial confirmations are held in a reconciliation queue.
Layer 4

Scale Architecture — Handling Peak Load

Paytm's transaction volume spikes sharply on festival days (Diwali, Dhanteras) and paydays. The infrastructure is designed around horizontal statelessness:

  • Stateless payment processors: The core payment microservices hold no session state. All state lives in Redis (hot path: idempotency keys, merchant cache) or the primary RDBMS (cold path: audit records). Services auto-scale behind a load balancer.
  • Event-driven settlement: After a payment success callback, settlement is handled asynchronously via a Kafka event. The settlement service consumes these events and updates ledger balances in batches, decoupling the synchronous payment path from the slower accounting path.
  • Read replicas for merchant resolution: The merchant profile store uses a leader-follower RDBMS setup. Payment flow reads go to followers; writes (KYC updates, account changes) go to the leader.
  • Circuit breakers on NPCI calls: If NPCI latency spikes, the circuit breaker opens and payments are fast-failed with a user-friendly "try again" message rather than piling up threads waiting on a slow upstream.
Layer 5

Settlement and Ledger Architecture

Paytm maintains a dual-ledger model: the wallet ledger for Paytm wallet balances and the bank passbook for UPI transactions that flow bank-to-bank through Paytm's VPA infrastructure.

Merchant settlements are batched — by default, a T+1 settlement cycle is used for small merchants (money hits their bank account next business day). Paytm Payment Bank or large enterprise merchants can access T+0 instant settlement for a fee.

The settlement pipeline is a classic event sourcing pattern: each payment event is immutable and appended to a log. Settlement totals are computed by replaying or aggregating the log, not by mutating balance rows in place. This makes the audit trail tamper-evident and rollback trivial.

JavaScript Example

Idempotency Key Generation and Lock Check

JavaScript Example 
import { createClient } from 'redis';
import { randomUUID } from 'crypto';

const redis = createClient({ url: process.env.REDIS_URL });
await redis.connect();

const LOCK_TTL_MS = 30_000; // 30 seconds — covers UPI round-trip + buffer

/**
 * Initiates a payment with idempotency protection.
 * Returns { initiated: true, key } or { initiated: false, reason }
 */
async function initiatePayment({ payerVpa, payeeVpa, amountPaise }) {
  const idempotencyKey = `pay:${payerVpa}:${payeeVpa}:${amountPaise}:${randomUUID()}`;

  // SET key "" EX ttl NX  — only succeeds if key does not exist
  const acquired = await redis.set(idempotencyKey, 'pending', {
    PX: LOCK_TTL_MS,
    NX: true,
  });

  if (!acquired) {
    return { initiated: false, reason: 'duplicate_request' };
  }

  try {
    // Fire-and-forget UPI call — result comes via NPCI callback
    const trn = await sendToUpiSwitch({ payerVpa, payeeVpa, amountPaise, idempotencyKey });

    // Update lock value to the TRN so reconciliation job can match callbacks
    await redis.set(idempotencyKey, trn, { PX: LOCK_TTL_MS, XX: true });

    return { initiated: true, key: idempotencyKey, trn };
  } catch (err) {
    // Release lock on client-side error so user can retry
    await redis.del(idempotencyKey);
    throw err;
  }
}

// Reconciliation: check pending payments older than 30s
async function reconcilePending(pendingKeys) {
  const results = await Promise.allSettled(
    pendingKeys.map(async (key) => {
      const trn = await redis.get(key);
      if (!trn || trn === 'pending') return;

      const status = await queryNpciStatus(trn);
      if (status === 'SUCCESS' || status === 'FAILURE') {
        await finaliseTransaction(trn, status);
        await redis.del(key);
      }
    })
  );
  return results.filter((r) => r.status === 'rejected').map((r) => r.reason);
}

async function sendToUpiSwitch(payload) { /* NPCI API call */ return 'TRN_' + randomUUID(); }
async function queryNpciStatus(trn) { return 'SUCCESS'; }
async function finaliseTransaction(trn, status) { console.log(trn, status); }
Practical checklist

Designing a QR payment system — what to nail

  • Separate QR generation (static/dynamic) from transaction processing — they have different lifecycles.
  • Use an idempotency key with a distributed lock before any external payment API call.
  • Always have a reconciliation job for the "callback never arrived" case.
  • Cache merchant/VPA resolution aggressively but with a short TTL (~60 s) to catch account suspensions.
  • Decouple settlement from payment confirmation — use an event queue (Kafka/SQS).
  • Put circuit breakers on every call to an external switch (NPCI, bank APIs).
  • Design for T+1 batch settlement as the default; T+0 as an opt-in premium tier.
FAQ

Common questions

Why not make all QRs dynamic to simplify server logic?

Dynamic QR requires a running POS device or smartphone. The bulk of Paytm's merchant base is micro-merchants who print a QR once and stick it on their stall. Static QR with server-side resolution is the only practical option at that scale.

What happens if NPCI is down?

Paytm queues the transaction in a durable store, shows the user a "payment processing" state, and retries when NPCI recovers. UPI mandates a 2-second response SLA; if that window expires, the transaction is auto-reversed by NPCI to prevent ambiguous states.

How does fraud detection work without slowing down the 2-second UPI window?

The risk engine runs in-process on the payment service, not as a remote call. Feature vectors (device fingerprint, velocity counters, location delta) are pre-computed and cached on the user's session. The rule evaluation is a fast in-memory lookup — typically under 10 ms.

How is the merchant settlement ledger kept consistent?

Event sourcing: every payment event is written to an immutable append-only log. Settlement balances are derived by aggregating this log, not by mutating balance rows. This makes the ledger auditable and makes rollback or correction trivially safe.

Key Takeaway

Paytm's QR architecture is a masterclass in separating concerns across time: QR generation is a one-time operation, merchant resolution is a read-heavy hot-path, payment routing is a latency-critical synchronous call, and settlement is a correctness-critical async process. Each of those layers scales and fails independently. The two design choices that enable everything else are stateless payment processors with Redis-backed idempotency and event-sourced ledgers with async settlement — together they deliver both the sub-2-second UPI response and the tamper-evident financial record.
