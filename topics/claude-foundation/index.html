Claude Foundation is the “how the machine behaves” half of AI fluency: four properties, each a continuum, that predict where an AI will help, where it will mislead, and what to do about it.

Core idea: Start with the four properties; when something breaks, name the two that collided and apply the matching fix.

Story · Why a “Foundation” track

Prompt tips age in months; mechanisms age in years. This track captures Claude Academy’s course on what generative AI can and can’t do, organised around four properties that explain most behaviour you will ever see. Each stage of the course has its own child page, so you can revisit exactly the property you are debugging.

Child topics

The seven child pages

  1. Intro & What We Mean by AI (Lessons 1–2, 6 min) — The 13-item roadmap, 4D pairing, generative vs classification AI, and the four properties.
  2. How AI Gets Its Character (Lesson 3, 5 min) — Pretraining vs fine-tuning and the four fingerprints: sycophancy, verbosity, over-caution, loose calibration.
  3. Next Token Prediction (Lessons 4–5, 9 min) — Autocomplete at scale, where fabrication clusters, Markov chain simulator, sampling knobs.
  4. Knowledge (Lessons 6–7, 8 min) — Training cutoffs, uneven coverage, embeddings and cosine-similarity retrieval.
  5. Working Memory (Lessons 8–9, 8 min) — Context window cliff, lost-in-the-middle effect, placing and repeating instructions.
  6. Steerability (Lessons 10–11, 7 min) — Word–intent gap, reasoning drift, letter vs spirit, the goal rewrite.
  7. When Properties Collide & Next Steps (Lessons 12–13, 8 min) — Diagnosing property collisions, one mental model, your weekly commitment, cheat sheet.

Total reading time ≈ 50 minutes, plus the hands-on exercises (about 5–10 minutes each).

The big picture

Four properties, four questions

PropertyQuestionCore mechanismWhere it breaks
Next Token PredictionWhere do answers come from?Continue the most likely patternSpecifics: names, dates, citations, URLs
KnowledgeWhat does it know?Frozen training snapshotRecent, rare, local, contested topics
Working MemoryWhat is it attending to?Fixed-size context windowLong inputs, mid-context details, new sessions
SteerabilityHow much am I in control?Instruction = pattern to continueLong reasoning, vague intent, exact maths

Plus the training “fingerprints” layered on top (sycophancy, verbosity, over-caution, loose calibration), and the key skill of diagnosing which two properties collided when something goes wrong.

Learning path

How to use the track

  • Read page 1 and build your task list (4–6 concrete AI tasks).
  • Work through pages 2–6 in order, running each page’s probes on your own tasks and annotating the list.
  • Finish with page 7 and write one concrete change for this week.
  • Afterwards, pair it with the human-competency course (AI Fluency Framework & Foundations) mentioned by the Academy.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Is this the official course text?

No. It is a paraphrased study guide built from the public lesson pages, with added runnable code and architect notes clearly labelled. For the original, use the source links.

Why seven child pages instead of thirteen?

Each “Try It Out” belongs with its lesson, and the two intro lessons share one concept, so the track maps to the course’s five stages without fragmenting.

Key Takeaway

Models change every quarter; the four properties don’t. Learn them once, keep a task list annotated against them, and your trust in AI becomes calibrated instead of hopeful or fearful.
