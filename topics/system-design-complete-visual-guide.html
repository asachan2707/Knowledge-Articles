Key Takeaways

  • Everything is a trade-off. Cost vs performance, reliability vs scalability, consistency vs availability — pick the tradeoff that fits your SLA and problem, not the one that sounds impressive.
  • Measure before you optimize. Pinterest's 99% build time reduction came from one config line — because they profiled first.
  • Simple scales when designed right. Stack Overflow runs on 9 servers with a monolith. Netflix uses GraphQL after starting with REST. Architecture evolves with load.
  • Security is not optional. Password salting, JWT verification, mTLS, DevSecOps — these are first-class system requirements, not afterthoughts.
  • The cloud is just someone else's computer. Understand the primitives (compute, storage, networking, messaging) before reaching for managed services.
  • Learn why each component exists. Start with a single server, understand why it breaks, add the right abstraction, and repeat. The engineer who can articulate the why behind every decision is the one who passes the interview and ships systems that last.
  • Internalize the trade-off vocabulary. CAP forces an explicit consistency-vs-availability choice during partitions, consistent hashing lets you add nodes without rehashing everything, and sliding-window rate limiting closes the fixed-window boundary exploit.
